# GrantCrest user guide

This guide is for grant writers, program staff and agency owners who use GrantCrest to find grants, write applications and prepare submission packages. It explains each screen and walks through each task in order. You do not need any technical knowledge. The same guide opens inside the app from **Help** in the left rail.

## What GrantCrest does and does not do

GrantCrest helps you find grants your organization may fit, write the application from facts you have checked, keep the budget and format in line with the funder's notice, collect approvals from the right people, and download a finished package.

It does **not** submit applications for you. Your authorized representative submits through Grants.gov Workspace or the funder's own portal. A match in GrantCrest is guidance about fit, never a decision that you are eligible: always read the funder's official notice. Foundation giving history shows who a foundation funded in the past; it is not a list of open calls.

> If your workspace shows "demo mode", no AI provider is set up yet. Drafting then adds a clearly labelled outline that you must replace with your own text before the package can be approved.

## The screens at a glance

The left rail is on every screen. On a phone it shrinks to icons; press and hold an icon to see its name.

| Rail item | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Grant Studio** | Your organizations, their grant matches, applications, deadlines and results. Most grant work starts here |
| **Grants** | A curated directory of grant and in-kind programs with their official sources |
| **Documents** | Long-form documents such as reports, guides and articles (the content features) |
| **Brands** | Voice and audience settings used by the content features |
| **Distribution** and **Performance** | Scheduling content and reading the results reported by connected channels |
| **Dashboard** | Content work in progress |
| **Settings** | Your team, two-factor sign-in, billing and plans, and workspace settings |
| **Help** | This guide |

The footer at the bottom of every page links back to this guide.

## Signing in

1. **The first time:** open the invitation link your workspace owner sent you. Enter your name, choose a password of at least 12 characters (a short sentence is easy to remember), and choose **Join**. The link works once and expires after 7 days.
2. **After that:** go to your GrantCrest address, enter your **email** and **password**, and choose **Sign in**.
3. You stay signed in on that browser for up to 7 days. On a shared computer, choose **Sign out** at the bottom of the left rail when you finish.

If you forget your password, ask an owner or admin of your workspace to remove you and invite you again; you choose a new password when you accept. To change a password you know, go to **Settings → Team → Your account**. Five wrong attempts pause sign-in from your network for 15 minutes.

## Workspaces and team roles

A workspace holds one team's organizations, applications and documents. Nobody outside the workspace can see its data. The bottom of the left rail shows which workspace you are in; if you belong to more than one, switch in **Settings → Team**.

Each person has a role, and GrantCrest shows only the buttons that role can use:

| Role | What they can do |
|---|---|
| **Owner** | Everything, including members, roles, billing and the workspace itself |
| **Admin** | Everything except removing owners and managing billing |
| **Writer** | Creates and edits applications and content, including budgets. Cannot approve sections or packages |
| **Reviewer** | Reads everything; reviews, approves and reopens sections |
| **Authorized representative** | Reads everything; approves sections and the final package, and records submissions and outcomes |
| **Finance** | Reads everything; edits budgets and records award amounts |
| **Viewer** | Read only |

**To invite someone (owners and admins):** go to **Settings → Team**, enter their email and role under **Invite someone**, and send them the link that appears. The link is shown only once. In the same place you can withdraw an invitation, change a role or remove a member. A removed member loses access at once. **Settings → Team → What each role can do** shows the full permission table, and the **audit log** lists sign-ins, security changes, member changes and approvals.

## Two-factor sign-in

Two-factor sign-in asks for a 6-digit code from your phone after your password, so a stolen password alone cannot open your account.

1. Go to **Settings → Team → Two-factor sign-in**.
2. Scan the code with an authenticator app such as 1Password, Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or Authy. The entry is named GrantCrest.
3. Enter the 6-digit code the app shows.
4. Copy or download your 10 recovery codes and keep them somewhere safe. Each works once if you lose your phone: choose **Use a recovery code** at sign-in.

Owners and admins can **require two-factor for everyone** under **Settings → Team → Members**. Anyone without it is asked to set it up before they can continue. **Where you're signed in** lists every browser signed in to your account; you can sign any of them out.

## Setting up an organization profile

An organization is the nonprofit or business that applies for grants. GrantCrest uses its profile to find and rank opportunities.

1. Open **Grant Studio** and choose **Add organization**.
2. Choose the organization type: 501(c)(3), other nonprofit, small business or other for-profit.
3. Enter the legal name, the state, the annual budget, the mission, and the focus areas separated by commas (for example `literacy, early childhood`). Specific focus areas give better matches than broad ones such as "health". Record the state: state programs only match organizations in that state.
4. Save. The organization page opens with tabs for **Matches**, **Readiness**, **Place designations**, **Applications**, **Fact library**, **Saved writing** and **Profile**.

**Readiness** tracks the registrations and documents funders ask for. Mark each one missing, in progress or ready, with its expiry date. Federal applications need an active SAM.gov registration (with a UEI) and a Grants.gov Authorized Organization Representative. Registration can take weeks, so start early. State programs do not need them.

**Place designations** records whether your address is in an Opportunity Zone, a HUBZone, a HUD Qualified Census Tract or Difficult Development Area, a persistent-poverty county or a USDA rural area. GrantCrest links to each agency's official lookup tool; you check there and record what it says and the date. Recheck every year.

## Confirming facts in the fact library

The fact library holds the facts funders ask for in every application: community need figures, programs, results, staff, partners and finances. **Drafting uses only facts you have confirmed**, so this step is what keeps the writing accurate.

1. Open the organization and choose **Fact library**.
2. Add a fact. Give its source (for example a report or your own records) and the date it is true as of.
3. Check it against the source, then tick the confirmation box.
4. If you edit a confirmed fact later, confirm it again.

When you write a section, GrantCrest lists the confirmed facts that fit it, each with a **Copy** button.

## Finding grants

### Federal and state opportunities (Grant Studio)

GrantCrest reads open opportunities from Grants.gov (federal) and the California Grants Portal (state) once a day. Each listing links to its official record.

1. Open the organization and choose **Matches**.
2. Each card shows a fit score, the reasons it fits, and what is missing. California programs carry a "CA state program" badge.
3. **Only my topics** hides opportunities you are eligible for but that are off your focus areas; clear it to see everything. **Only new** shows what arrived since you last looked; **Mark all as seen** resets the count.
4. Open an opportunity to read its summary, then open the official notice before you decide.

The Grant Studio page shows each organization's new matches and those closing within 14 days. **Add to calendar** on an opportunity or application downloads a calendar entry with reminders, and **Deadline calendar** downloads every open application.

### The curated directory (Grants)

**Grants** in the left rail is a hand-checked directory of grant and in-kind programs, including corporate and community programs. Search by mission or expense, then filter by funding type, location and status. Location is a search filter, not a decision that you qualify. Listings older than their review date show "Review required".

### Foundations

**Grant Studio → Foundations** shows which private foundations gave money, how much, to whom and where, from the grants they report on their IRS tax filings. Search by funder name, cause or the state they gave in, then open a funder to see giving by year, cause and state, and their largest recent grants. Use it to research who funds work like yours. It is past giving, not an open call: check the funder's own website for how and when to apply.

## Starting an application

1. From an opportunity, choose **Start application**.
2. Confirm that you have read the notice's eligibility section and that your organization meets it. GrantCrest records who confirmed it and when.
3. The application opens on five steps: **Requirements**, **Write**, **Budget**, **Format & letters** and **Review & package**.

## The notice PDF checklist

The funder's notice (the NOFO or request for proposals) says what the application must contain. The checklist turns it into rows you can tick off.

1. On the **Requirements** step, find **Checklist from the notice**.
2. Choose **Attach notice PDF** and pick the file, or paste a link to the notice PDF and choose **Fetch**.
3. GrantCrest reads the pages and proposes rows: sections, review criteria, attachments, forms, eligibility rules, formatting and deadlines. Progress shows on screen.
4. Every proposed row quotes the notice and names its page. Open the page to see the quotation highlighted.
5. For each row, choose **Confirm**, edit it, or reject it. Nothing is confirmed for you. **Confirm all** confirms the rows that are left, skipping any you rejected.

If the notice is a scanned image, GrantCrest cannot read it; add the requirements by hand. Attaching another notice replaces the current one. You can also add any requirement yourself.

## Drafting with confirmed facts

On the **Write** step, work through each section within its word or character limit.

- **Draft with AI** writes a first draft from your organization's confirmed facts only. Anything it cannot support is marked `[Add: …]`; fill each one with a sourced fact. The package cannot be approved while any `[Add: …]` or `TBD` remains.
- **Review** scores the section against the notice's review criteria.
- **Approve** (reviewers, authorized representatives, owners and admins) locks the section. Editing it later reopens it.
- **Save to library** keeps an approved section for later applications. Next time, **Reuse approved writing** lets you replace or append it, then adapt it to the new funder.
- **Writing checks** under the editor list placeholders still to fill, very long sentences, the reading grade and, for objectives, whether each has a number, a time frame and a starting point.
- On a logic model section, the **Logic model builder** takes inputs, activities, outputs and outcomes (one per line) and builds the section.

## Budgets

On the **Budget** step, add a line for each cost with its category and a justification. Set your indirect cost rate if you have one. GrantCrest totals the budget and checks it against the award ceiling. Writers, finance and owners and admins can edit the budget.

## Format and letters of support

On **Format & letters**, copy the notice's font, size, line spacing, margins, page limit and required file name. The page estimate and the downloaded file follow them, and the file name is checked against Grants.gov's naming rules. Add each letter of support you need, with who you asked and when it is due. **Copy request** gives you a request to edit and send yourself; mark each letter received or declined.

## Approvals

GrantCrest records who approved what and when:

1. A reviewer or authorized representative approves each section on the **Write** step.
2. When every check on **Review & package** passes, the **authorized representative** approves the package.
3. Any later change to a section, requirement, budget or format withdraws the package approval, and the application returns to draft until it is approved again.

GrantCrest never approves on anyone's behalf.

## Exporting the package

On **Review & package**:

1. Read the **Competitiveness rating** (0 to 100). It shows how ready the application is to compete: fit, reviewer scores weighted by the notice's points, completeness, and registrations and time. **To raise it** lists what is worth the most points. It is not a chance of winning.
2. Fix each failing check; every one has a **Fix** link to the right place.
3. **Download draft** gives a Word file for team review at any time.
4. Once the authorized representative chooses **Approve package**, choose **Download final package** (a Word file in the notice's format).
5. Submit it yourself through Grants.gov Workspace or the funder's portal, using the forms the package lists.
6. Back on the same step, use **Outcome** to record the submission date and tracking number, and later the award, decline or withdrawal. The Grant Studio page then shows your pipeline and award rate.

## Billing and plans

Owners manage billing in **Settings → Billing**. If it says **Billing is off**, nothing is limited and nobody is charged.

When billing is on, every plan starts with a 14-day free trial with no card needed, and yearly billing gives two months free. Plans differ by the number of users, organizations and AI-drafted applications a month; the Billing page shows the current prices, limits and your usage. **Manage billing** opens the payment page, where you change your plan, update your card or cancel. Expert review (a grant writer reviews one AI-drafted application) is bought per application on the same page.

## Getting help

- Open **Help** in the left rail, or **Help and user guide** in the footer, to read this guide in the app.
- For access changes (a new member, a lost phone without recovery codes, a locked account), ask an owner or admin of your workspace.
- For anything else, contact your GrantCrest administrator. Please describe what you were doing, the page, the time, and any message shown. Never send passwords or recovery codes.

Common messages:

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Too many sign-in attempts" | Wait 15 minutes, then sign in with the correct password |
| "That code is not correct" | Set your phone's clock to automatic and enter the next code, or use a recovery code |
| "This workspace requires two-factor sign-in" | Follow the setup screen; it takes about a minute |
| "Demo mode" or `[DEMO OUTPUT …]` | No AI provider is set up yet. Ask your administrator |
| A button you expect is missing | Your role cannot use it. Ask an owner or admin |
| "Could not reach GrantCrest" | Check your internet connection and try again |
